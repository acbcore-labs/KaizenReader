# The Complete Book of Enoch

*Translated by R.H. Charles, D.Litt., D.D.*

## Introduction to the Pseudepigrapha

The Book of Enoch is an ancient Hebrew apocalyptic religious text ascribed by tradition to Enoch, the great-grandfather of Noah. Although not regarded as scripture by modern canons, its impact on the development of early theological thought was profound.

The patristic fathers frequently quoted its passages with deep reverence. In his early writings, Origen (Comm. in Matt. 10.18; p. 13.881) references these sacred texts, while Tertullian (De Cultu Fem. i. 3) defends their antiquity against skepticism. The historical chronicle traces kings like Manasseh, whose reign reflected the complex spiritual struggles of the era.

## Section I. Chapters I-XXXVI: The Book of the Watchers

Ch. 1: The word of the blessing of Enoch, wherewith he blessed the elect and righteous, who will be living in the day of tribulation.

In those days, the holy angels Uriel, Raphael, and Phanuel looked down from heaven. Meanwhile, Samyaza, their leader, bound his associates upon Mount Hermon, alongside Azazel and Baraqiel. And Methuselah, the son of Enoch, preserved the testimony for generations yet unborn.
